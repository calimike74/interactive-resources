**Date:** 1 September 2026. **Status:** built to the gate; the 2D treatment of Inside the Synthesiser. Commissioned by Mike the same day ("ok lets start with ... Synth and Compressor to 2D") from the 31 Aug estate verdict: a synthesiser is a panel of controls, and a panel of controls fails the internal / spatial rule that keeps a thing in 3D. ## 1. What the topic is, and what the exam does with it

Synthesis is the paper's own drawing of an instrument: oscillators into a filter into an amplifier, an envelope asking, an LFO moving something too slowly to hear. The written paper asks it two ways. The practical tasks name settings and mark them one at a time: two square oscillators, detuned, filtered, in the right octave (2023 AS Q3(a): "Square wave (1); Detune added, suitable amount (1); Correct filter setting (1); Correct octave (both oscillators) (1)"); two saws, slightly detuned, the same octave, a low-pass no brighter than the example (2024 AS Q2(c)); a lead that is square, mono, with a subtle portamento, a soft attack, full sustain, a short release, a muted cutoff with no resonance and no filter envelope, and an LFO wobbling the cutoff (2025 Q3(a)); the fills with "A=0, D=max, S=max, R=enough release so that the drop in octave is heard" (2023 Q2(d); 2020 Q2(b)). And Q6 shows an unfamiliar 1982 synthesiser and asks whether its settings suit a job: a pad (2019), a bass (2024), for 20 marks.

The reports say where the marks go. 2024: "Candidates who divided up their writing into subheadings, one for each synthesiser section, provided the most concise and structured writing yielding highest marks"; "The most common AO4 marks were for describing the fast attack and release"; "Very commonly, candidates mistakenly thought that the LFO was something audible rather than a control signal"; "many learners misidentified it as a boost/cut rather than an LPF and would discuss what resonance was but didn't discuss its impact on the sound". 2019: "only the top performing candidates noticed that the envelope parameters were routed to the filter cutoff and not the amplitude". 2023 AS: the common issues were no detune and the wrong octave, one octave too high. 2022 AS: a monophonic patch "did not play the complete chords". 2020: "Only the best candidates noticed that the release was very short".

## 2. The judgement: the console is the panel, the stage is three pictures

The console is the playable panel the verdict demanded, in the paper's own sections: Part (Bass, Pad, Lead, Keys), Oscillators (wave, octave, Detune), Filter (LPF / HPF / BPF, Cutoff, Resonance, Env), Envelope (A, D, S, R as four dials), LFO (Pitch / Cutoff / Amp, Rate, Depth); the More row adds Voices (Poly / Mono), Glide (Off / Subtle / Long), Osc 2 (Pair / Sub / Off) and the LFO's shape. Every dial is a drag target, as the 3D's knobs were. Hold plays the oscillators raw: the filter open, the envelope a switch, the LFO off, so what the rest of the panel takes away is heard.

- **The voice** (`lib/bench/synth-model.js`, `SynthBench.jsx`): oscillators, the one subject the Bench Standard admits them for (§3 law 5), declared with `synthesis`. Two per note (a detuned pair, or a square sub an octave down, or one), a BiquadFilterNode, a GainNode the envelope drives, a tremolo gain, one LFO OscillatorNode for the bench feeding three gains (cents into the oscillators' detune, cents into the filter's detune, a fraction into the tremolo). The amplitude envelope is booked on `amp.gain` and the filter envelope on `filter.detune`, so Cutoff moves live on `filter.frequency` and the LFO adds into the same param.
- **What is drawn is what is heard.** The harmonic series per wave is the band-limited series OscillatorNode makes (square odd 1/n, saw all 1/n, triangle odd 1/n², sine the fundamental). The filter curve is the RBJ biquad BiquadFilterNode runs (`filterCoefficients`, with the EQ bench's `sectionResponse`), and resonance is written the way Web Audio takes it: in dB for lowpass and highpass (0 % is the Butterworth corner at -3 dB, 100 % a 24 dB peak), as Q for bandpass. The envelope is four straight lines (`adsrAt`) and the graph books the same lines with linear ramps; a key that lifts mid-attack releases from the model's value at that instant.
- **The parts are the jobs.** Four note lists at 100 bpm in A minor: a bass in quavers, a pad in whole-bar chords, a lead melody, a keyboard part in stabs. The part a patch plays is the job the paper asks about, so a pad patch can be heard on the bass, which is what Q6 asks a candidate to judge. In Mono, a chord keeps its top note only, the 2022 fault.
- **The dot is the dial** (law 25, `check-bench`): the gold curve on the harmonics screen is the filter, its dot at the cutoff; drag across and the Cutoff dial follows, drag up and the resonance rises (the dot's height is the peak in dB). `data-cutoff` on the canvas equals the console's. The keys at the foot of the stage play the voice, and so do A to K; pressing the C key makes the stage report a note (`data-note`).

## 3. Three jobs, three pictures

- **Core, `data-stage=scope`:** three tiles across the top (OSCILLATORS, FILTER, AMPLIFIER; the one last touched is lit); the WAVE screen with two cycles leaving the synth, the pre-filter wave dotted behind it; the HARMONICS screen with the two oscillators' lines on a log axis and the filter drawn over them in gold with its draggable dot; the keyboard. The line names what you hear (the part, the waves and how far apart, the filter and its brightness word, how the note starts and stops, the LFO) and says what to try.
- **A-level, `data-stage=sections`:** the paper's drawing. VCO, VCF and VCA across in signal order with OUT, ENV and LFO beneath, control routes dashed when not routed, each box carrying its settings in the paper's words and a verdict for the part it is playing (suits, partly, does not suit). Touch a box and the line judges that section: AO3 the setting and its effect, AO4 the verdict, the better setting, and the report that says so. With a paper's task set, the line is the scheme's own points with its year, "as directed" or "not yet: <the point missed>".
- **Extension, `data-stage=machine`:** one note in time on five lanes: GATE (key down for the part's note length, key up), ENVELOPE (asks: the four straight lines with A, D, S and R marked), AMPLIFIER (obeys: the note's shape, with the tremolo if the LFO is on the level), CUTOFF (obeys the envelope's lift and the LFO's wobble, on a log axis), LFO (drawn to scale, one cycle labelled, never heard). A playhead crosses it while the bench plays. The line opens the machine: the envelope as a control signal, the filter routing the 2019 report names, the LFO as a wave too slow to hear.

## 6b. Measured (`scripts/measure-synth.mjs`, 1 Sep, headless Chromium at 48 kHz)

Two analysers in front of the destination: a short one (10.7 ms) for level, a long one (170 ms) for pitch by a 0.5 Hz DFT sweep with an octave check; the keys pressed by their `data-key` position.

- **Level.** The six presets between -15 and -22 dB mean (2023 paper -15.1, 2024 -16.2, 2025 -18.7, Fills -15.6, Judge: a bass -19.0, Judge: a pad -21.8). A held C2 on each pair through the 700 Hz low-pass: square -22.2, saw -19.9, triangle -23.4, sine -23.4; the same with the filter open, to 0.1 dB, because a 65 Hz note's energy is below the cutoff whatever the wave. The oscillator gains were retuned from the Oscilloscope's (sine 0.35 to 0.27, triangle 0.42 to 0.33, saw 0.42 to 0.55) after a first pass ran the sine pair 7 dB over the saw pair.
- **Pitch.** The C key on the bass: 65.5 Hz at octave 0, 131.0 at +1, 32.5 at -1 (65.4, 130.8, 32.7); on the lead, 261.5 (261.6). A first pass read 49 Hz at octave 0: the bass's last quaver, still booked when Stop faded the master, came back the moment a key raised it. Stop now cuts every booked voice (`clear({ keys: false })`).
- **Detune.** A held C2 pair at 0 cents: the level steady within 0.2 dB. At 50 cents: a 38 dB swing, the beat of the fundamentals at 1.9 Hz with the third harmonic's at 5.7 riding on it.
- **Filter.** The bass's spectral centroid through a 700 Hz low-pass 118 Hz, through a 700 Hz high-pass 1168 Hz: the high-pass removes the bass.
- **Envelope.** A held key with A 600, D 400, S 80, R 900: -32.9 dB at 50 ms, -22.0 at 150, -19.5 at 300, -15.0 at 600, -16.3 at 1000 (the decay to 80 %). With A 5, D 250, S 50, R 90: -12.0 at 20 ms, -18.8 at 300, -20.0 at 600 (8 dB down, the sustain at half). The 900 ms release traced every 100 ms after key-up reaches silence at 900 ms; the early samples wobble 2 dB around the model's straight line because the pair beats.
- **LFO.** Tremolo at 4 Hz, depth 100 %, on the pad: 14 swells in 3 s (12 expected), the level to silence at each trough.
- **Mono.** The pad chord in Mono -28.7 dB, in Poly -21.7: four notes against one. A first pass read the two the same, because Mono was not thinning the sequenced chord; it now keeps the top note of each step, the 2022 fault.
- **The dials.** A log dial's keyboard step could not leave its minimum (1 ms attack, 0.1 Hz rate) because the setters rounded to a unit coarser than half a position; they now keep three significant figures.

## 8. Held, and what is not built

- **FM is not here.** Operator stays with its own card; a method chip can join the bench later.
- **Velocity-sensitive filtering** (2020 and 2023: bright on the loud bars, filtered on the quiet ones) is not modelled: the parts carry no velocities. The Fills preset marks wave, envelope and filter, and says so.
- **Pulse width and PWM**: built on 2 Sep (§10); the line below is the 1 Sep position, kept for the record. They were not on the panel: OscillatorNode has no pulse width. The detuned pair is the same movement by another route, and the Reference said so.
- **Pitch bend** stays on the Piano Roll and the retired MIDI controller; the Reference says so and gives the papers' ranges.
- **Envelope to pitch** (the spec's "mapping envelope ... to ... pitch", the 2025 Q6 tom) is not routed: the envelope reaches the amplifier and the cutoff, the LFO reaches the pitch. The Reference names it as the one routing written, not played.
- **White noise** (on the spec; the 2025 Q6 snare and hi-hat): built on 2 Sep as a source in the mixer (§10). The 1 Sep position: it sat with the console-as-panel proposal of that evening, awaiting Mike's answer.
- **Sine and triangle** left the panel on 2 Sep: the paper's 1982 synthesiser has neither, the Oscilloscope bench (2.5) draws all four with sound, and the Reference says where they went.

## 10. The panel (2 Sep 2026): the console re-cut as the paper's, on Mike's steer

**The steer.** On the evening of 1 Sep Mike sent a picture of a 1982-style monophonic synthesiser plugin (LFO · VCO · SOURCE MIXER · VCF · VCA · ENV, sliders, a keyboard) "for inspiration"; a sheet put the morning's dial console beside the same bench re-cut as that panel and asked one question, "panel" or "labels only". His answer on 2 Sep: "thats the one-brilliant. I like the PROPOSED: SLIDERS which are more common in a DAW lets see how this look, after you changed this just deploy". This section is that build.

**What changed, and why each thing is on the panel.** The two Q6 reports name exactly the three things the panel adds, in their own words, so the re-cut is not a skin:
- **The console is the paper's six sections, in its order, with its labels**: LFO · VCO · SOURCE MIXER · VCF · VCA · ENV, then Hear. The 2024 report: "Many candidates did not identify what LFO/VCO/VCA/VCF represented and simply used the label from the synth". Every control is a slider (the kit's Fader, slim, with its value above and its name below), which is also what a DAW's synth shows.
- **A source mixer, mixed rather than chosen**: Pulse, Saw, Sub (a square an octave or two down; the octave in the More row) and Noise, each at a level. The 2024 report: "Candidates were often successful in discussing the sub-oscillator". The spec's "selecting and mixing" is this row. Sine and triangle are not on the panel (the paper's synthesiser has neither); the Reference sends them to the Oscilloscope bench.
- **Pulse width, and PW by LFO**: Width narrows the pulse from a square (50 %); PW by LFO lets the LFO swing it between the width and its mirror. The 2019 report: "many candidates thought that this was a square wave and did not appreciate that the pulse width was being modulated by the LFO"; the 2024 report: "It was very rare to see candidates that fully understood that the LFO was controlling the pulse width modulation of a pulse wave". On the Core stage the WAVE screen breathes and the HARMONICS screen's even harmonics come and go, live, at the LFO's phase; on the A-level drawing a PW route runs from the LFO to the VCO; the Extension LFO lane runs when PW is by LFO even at zero depth.
- **A VCA switched between Env and Gate**: on Gate the amplifier follows the key alone, full while it is down and nothing after, the envelope disabled (it still reaches the cutoff if Env in the VCF says so). The 2019 report: candidates confused "the VCA gate (disabling the envelope) with a noise gate designed to cut out background noise". The env → VCA route on the A-level drawing goes dashed; the Extension AMPLIFIER lane becomes a rectangle under an envelope that still asks.
- **Range in the paper's feet**: 16' · 8' · 4', 8' the part's own octave; the bench says both.
- **Part moved to the bar** beside the presets (the panel has no part), and LFO shape moved from More to the LFO section as the paper has it. The More row: Voices, Glide, Osc 2 (Pair / Fifth / Off; the sub left it for the mixer), Sub octave, Arp.

**What is drawn is what sounds, now with a mixer.** The series are in the amplitude a wave of peak 1 really has (saw 2/nπ, square 4/nπ odd, pulse (4/nπ)·|sin nπw|), scaled by each source's level and `SOURCE_GAIN`, the same numbers the graph runs, so a square and a saw at the same slider draw and sound in the same proportion. The noise is a floor across the HARMONICS screen shaped by the filter's own curve (take the pulse and saw to zero and the screen shows the filter as the sound). The judge's oscillator section, the schemes' "square wave" and "two sawtooth oscillators" points and the hearing line all read the mix (`waveOf`, `sourceSaid`, `pure`): a saw in the mix, noise, a narrow pulse or PW by LFO each lose the 2023 mark with its own sentence; a sub beneath keeps it (a square too).

**The graph.** One saw oscillator per VCO serves both the saw and the pulse: the pulse is the saw minus itself delayed by the width (a DelayNode), so both are band-limited, and PW by LFO is the shared LFO into a per-voice gain on that delay's time (the swing in seconds is (0.5 − width) × the period). A Pair or a Fifth is a second such VCO. The sub is a square OscillatorNode; the noise a looped two-second AudioBuffer of Math.random, one per graph. The VCA on Gate books a 4 ms rise and a 6 ms fall on `amp.gain` instead of the envelope; the filter envelope is booked as before.

**Gates.** check-bench 40/40 in Chromium and WebKit, local, unchanged fixture (law 25's dot is now the Cutoff slider; the Judge preset still lands Attack at 600 ms). Tests 334/334 (the two synth files rewritten for the mixer: 22 + 6).

**Measured** (`scripts/measure-synth.mjs`, 2 Sep, headless Chromium at 48 kHz; the harness gained `pwm` and `gate`, and `level` now reads each source):
- A held C2 with the filter open, each source alone at 100 %, paired: pulse −21.2, saw −19.9, sub −19.7, noise −21.7 dB RMS (within 2 dB; noise raised from 0.12 to 0.3 after the first run put it 7 dB under). Through the LPF at 700 Hz the three pitched sources read the same to 0.1 dB (their energy sits under the cutoff) and the noise drops to −33.9 dB: the filter removing the broadband source, which the HARMONICS screen shows as the floor following the curve.
- The pulse at 5 % width, filter open: −27.4 dB, 6 dB under the square (physics gives 2√(w(1−w)) = −7.2 dB).
- Range 8' · 4' · 16' on the bass's C key: 65.5 · 131.0 · 32.5 Hz. The sub alone at 1 oct · 2 oct: 32.5 · 16.5 Hz.
- PWM, width 5 % by hand: the level holds within 1.3 dB over 3 s (in 100 ms bins). By the LFO at 1.0 Hz: −24.5 to −18.4 dB, six swells in 3 s, exactly the two per cycle a 5-to-95 % sweep gives.
- The VCA, on the Judge bass's A 600 ms / R 900 ms envelope: on Env, −45.1 dB at 15 ms and −25.0 at 100 ms after key-down (the attack rising), −16.7 and −17.9 at 50 and 300 ms after key-up (the release holding). On Gate, −8.8 dB at 15 ms and −9.7 at 100 ms (full at once), silence by 50 ms after key-up.
- Unchanged from 1 Sep: presets −14.4 to −17.6 dB mean; a 50 ct pair beats 39 dB deep; LPF vs HPF at 700 Hz centroid 123 vs 1300 Hz; tremolo at 4 Hz 14 swells in 3 s; Mono −16.8 vs Poly −10.5 dB on the pad chord; the envelope and release traces follow the model's lines.

**What cost a round.**
1. Part in the bar as a third column took the bench's line down to 400 px: nine lines at A-level in Teacher mode, and the stage collapsed to 164 px. Part and the presets now share one wrapping block (max 504 px, two rows at 1280), the line keeps ~680 px.
2. The Hear column's stat spans were inline in a block with the section's 1.5 line-height, so five stats were 189 px tall and the console overflowed by 5 px in both engines; blocks with line-height 1.1 fixed it. A slider column is 38 px wide (value 9.5 px, name 8.5 px mono); the ENV's four run at 40 px for "Sustain" and "Release".
3. A 10 ms RMS window straddles a narrow pulse, so "PWM by hand" read a 14 dB swing that was not there; the harness bins to 100 ms for that scenario.
4. The pitch detector's 25 Hz floor reported the sub two octaves down as 49 Hz (its third harmonic); the floor is 14 Hz now.
5. Extension's summary line ran to eight lines at 700 px with the More row open; it is now the envelope plus whichever of the LFO and the filter has something to say, and a lane under 30 px keeps its name and drops its note.

**Held (still).** FM; velocity-sensitive filtering; pitch bend (the Piano Roll); envelope to pitch (the Reference names it). The 2024 report's master pitch / tuning point and its "pushing the pitchbend wheel forward would yield vibrato" are not on the panel.


## 11. Shapes and sounds (2 Sep 2026, afternoon)

Mike walked the panel ("it's fine") and asked for two things: the old bench's sine and triangle back ("the visual of that was quite cool ... can we get that back again"), and presets that are sounds ("a pad preset ... a synth stab preset ... the students recognise the differences in the ADSR"), with the three paper presets no longer leading. Both agreed 2 Sep, built and deployed the same afternoon.

**The shape switch.** The mixer keeps four sliders. The second slider's name is now a switch: press it and the wave steps Saw, Tri, Sine (`shape` in the model, `setShape`, the button under the slider carries `data-shape`). Nothing on the console moves, which is why it is the name and not a chip row (the console is 1280 px wide at 1280 exactly). The spec asks for "selecting and mixing sine, triangle, pulse, square and saw"; the 2025 scheme reads the shape ("Allow saw, pulse or triangle. Not sine"), and the bench now says so: a triangle lead passes the wave point, a sine loses it "which the scheme rules out"; the 2024 keyboard's "two sawtooth oscillators" fails on a triangle with its own sentence. The judge treats a sine as partly for every part ("one harmonic, so the filter has nothing to take away; subtractive synthesis starts from a richer wave") and a triangle bass as partly (faint odd harmonics, soft). The WAVE screen draws the triangle with its alternating series (`triCoef`, `shapeCoef`); the bars use the sizes. The graph runs a second OscillatorNode of the chosen type per VCO beside the saw that makes the pulse (one saw cannot be both), and switches its type live.

**Sounds first.** Four presets at Core, `level: 'core'`: Synth bass (a saw with a sub, a detuned pair, LPF the envelope opens, A 3 ms R 90 ms, mono), Synth pad (saw plus a pulse the LFO breathes at 0.4 Hz, 12 ct pair, A 600 ms S 85 % R 1.2 s, poly), Synth stab (two saws on the keyboard part's chords, A 2 ms D 160 ms S 0 R 150 ms, Env 50 % on the filter), Synth lead (a square, mono, subtle glide, vibrato 5 Hz 12 %). Each suits its part in every section, the unit test says so, and each has its own next move (Attack to 5 ms turns the pad into a stab). The papers, Fills and the two Judge patches are the A-level and Extension presets (`presetsFor(depth)`), where the paper's job lives; the state carries across the levels, so a sound pressed at Core is judged at A-level. The default patch is now the Synth bass, and the harness and the gate press the papers at A-level.

**Measured (localhost, C2 held, paired, filter open, `scripts/measure-synth.mjs level`).** tri alone −19.0 dB mean, sine alone −19.0, saw alone −19.9 (gains tri 0.55, sine 0.45 against the saw's 0.55); the four sources unchanged (pulse −21.2, saw −19.9, sub −19.7, noise −21.7). Tests 337. check-bench clear in Chromium and WebKit at 1280 × 700 and 1440 × 900.

**What cost a round.**
1. The bass sound's 3 ms attack hydrated as two different `aria-valuenow` strings: Node's and Chromium's `Math.log` differ in the last digit. The log fader rounds its position to four places now.
2. With the LFO pointed at nothing and PW by LFO on, the LFO's meaning line read "offPW"; it reads "PW · a control signal".
