# The Piano Roll (1.5): design record

**Date:** 30 August 2026. **Status:** built and live at `resources.musictechstudio.co.uk/piano-roll`, seventh bench to the Bench Standard. Built on Mike's "ok I think we roughly have this moving forward and I need to go; can you start working on the next few while I'm away ... focus on areas where we need to get an explore on", after the recommendation of 1.5 as the topic with the most marks and nothing at the bar.

## 1. What the topic is, and what the exam does with it

Sequencing is MIDI: messages that say which note, how hard, when, on which channel, and what else changed. The spec (9MT0 area 1.5) names real-time and non-real-time input, quantise (hard values, swing and percentage), editing (velocity, note length, the piano roll, the list editor) and the data itself (note on and off, pitch, controllers, pitch bend, LSB and MSB, tempo). The practical paper opens every year with a MIDI file on the bench, and the questions are the same questions: the velocity table in decimal and in binary (every year since 2019), the notes assigned to the wrong drum sounds (2019, 2021, 2024, 2025), the most appropriate quantise value (2021, 2022, 2023), the bar a 1/16 quantise would break (2020), the pitch bend range (2020, 2021, 2022, 2023, 2026), draw the part on the piano roll (2019, 2020, 2021, 2023, 2025), the playback head's position in bar, beat, division and tick (2021), why 127 is the ceiling (2021, 2026), how many bytes a bend takes and where its centre is (2023, 2024). Mike's own 1.5 note played the topic down ("not a major topic, fine with one or two"); the papers say otherwise, and the difficulties file has it "critical, almost every year".

The band had the Beat Machine (a hub, and good) and the MIDI Pitch Bend & Controller page, which Mike called three resources in one with scrolling that was infuriating. That page is retired to a stub (WO-08 pattern) and its one good idea, the think-then-reveal question, lives in the bench's Teacher tab.

## 2. The judgement: the MIDI file, on the roll

The picture is the one every student already has: the piano roll with the velocity lane beneath it, as a DAW draws them. One part plays at a time, as the paper bounces it: the drums ("drums.mid", the paper's Question 2) on an acoustic kit or a drum machine, or the bass (the paper's Question 1) on a square-wave synth. The notes are the control.

- **The model is a note list** (`lib/bench/midi-model.js`): every note a recorded position in beats, a length, a note number and a velocity. Quantise is non-destructive: `placedTime` moves each note toward the grid by the strength, so Off restores the take and a moved note draws a dashed ghost where it was played. Times read as bar, beat, division and tick at 960 to the quarter (Logic's, and the 2021 paper's format). Velocity in binary is seven digits with the leading zeros the schemes allow.
- **The drum map is the task.** Note → sound, on the General MIDI notes (36 kick, 38 snare, 42 closed hat, 46 open hat, 49 crash, 51 ride, 48 high tom, 45 low tom). The papers' "assigned to the incorrect sounds" preset scrambles it; the fix is on the kit (the Sound chips for the selected row), never on the roll, which is what "you should not change the rhythm" means.
- **The bend lane is data, the range is the synth's.** The bass file carries one pitch bend in bar 4 (centre to full downward, −8192, and back over two beats). The Bend section sets the synth's range (2, 7, 12 or 24 semitones), so the same data are a tone or an octave: the 2022 scheme's "changes vox pitch in some small way, i.e. 2 semitones" is one mark of three, "matches" is three.
- **The note on the roll is the control** (law 22, `check-bench`): drag a block in time (snapped to the grid, a 32nd with the grid off) or onto another row (a lane of the kit, a key of the bass's range), drag its bar in the velocity lane, click an empty cell to add a note, double-click to remove. `data-note` is the selected note's number, the console's Note readout carries the same number (`data-note-number`), dragging the block up raises both and `data-roll` changes; the Velocity dial at End lands the stage at 127.
- **Hold plays the example.** The paper's example.wav is what the file should sound like: the right sounds, the right range, every note. `referenceOf` builds it from the preset; the scheduler books a beat at a time (`beatsPerBar: 1`, 96 bpm), so a hold or an edit lands within a beat rather than a bar.
- **The engine.** Drums are one-shots through `playBuffer` with a velocity curve (`(vel / 127) ^ 1.6`). The bass is a voice a note at a time: a square-wave oscillator into a low-pass filter whose peak follows the velocity (500 Hz plus up to 3.6 kHz, closing to a floor over 320 ms: the papers' "velocity sensitive filtering" and filter envelope), an amplitude envelope with a 6 ms attack and an 18 ms release, and the bend lane sampled every 32nd of a beat across the note into `detune` (cents) with `setValueCurveAtTime`. The bench declares `synthesis`: the papers' own part is a synth (2020, 2023, 2026: "use a square wave", "match the pitch bend range", "copy the velocity sensitive filtering"), the one place the standard admits an oscillator.

## 3. Three jobs, three pictures

- **Core, `data-stage=roll`:** the roll (drum rows named by the sound they play, bass rows by note name with black keys shaded), the grid at the quantise value with beats and bars stronger, notes as blocks in the part's colour paled by their velocity, the dashed ghost where a quantised note was played, the velocity lane with a bar and a dot per note (or the bend lane, teal, when the bass's lower lane is switched to Bend), the playhead with the position read as bar beat div tick in the title row. The line names what the file is doing (the kit, the feel, the loudest and quietest hit in bar 2) and says what to try.
- **A-level, `data-stage=list`:** the list editor beside the roll, as the paper's own table: the other messages first (tempo, time signature, key, track name, instrument name: the 2019 scheme's list), then every note of the bar under the playhead as a row with its position, Note On, note name and number, velocity in decimal and in binary, the selected note's row lit gold; pitch bend rows for the bass. Faults marked where they are: a coral tag on every row that plays the wrong sound ("wants kick"), a coral outline on hits that share a step, the bar the task names shaded, the table's two values labelled in the lane. The judge answers the paper's question about what is loaded, AO3 then AO4, quoting a scheme line with its year; no numeric marks.
- **Extension, `data-stage=wire`:** the wire under the roll: the selected note's three bytes with the first bit of each in colour (gold for the status byte's 1, teal for a data byte's 0) and the hex and decimal beside them, and the last message sent as the loop plays. The line explains the flag bit, 2^7, and the pitch bend's two data bytes with both displays of the same value (−8192 to 8191 from the centre; 0 to 16383 from the bottom, the 2024 paper's).

## 4. The papers' questions as presets

Seven, in pairs where the paper has a fault: **Velocity table** (the file as handed over; the table for bar 2), **Wrong sounds** (the 2019 map, seven rows wrong), **Hi-hat roll** (a 1/32 roll in bars 2 and 4; the answer is 1/32 and a 1/16 quantise stacks it), **Played** (the drums as a drummer played them, every hit up to 28 ms off; the 2025 feel comparison at strength 0, 50 and 100), **Triplet trap** (bar 2 in eighth-note triplets on a line in quarters; the 2020 bar), **Bend range** (the octave fall with the synth left at 2), **Draw the part** (bar 1 given, bar 2 empty, the example to hold). Every preset is pinned to its verdict by a test (`tests/bench-midi-model.test.mjs`), and every judge line to its shape (`tests/bench-midi-depth.test.mjs`).

## 5. Audio

A new bench brings its own audio (Mike, 28 Aug): two drum kits generated on Mike's ElevenLabs account on 30 Aug, an acoustic rock kit and a 1980s drum machine, eight sounds each (the drum-map task's list: kick, snare, closed and open hi-hat, ride, crash, two toms). ElevenLabs returns several hits filling the requested length, so the pipeline (`scratchpad midi/gen.mjs`, `qc2.py`) measured every take (peak, first-hit length, ring to −54 dB, spectral centroid against a band per sound), cut each to its first hit 3 ms before the onset, sealed the edges and normalised to −3 dBFS. The acoustic kick took three batches of prompts before a take passed; the electronic low tom and closed hat took a second. Credited under `midi/` in `docs/audio-credits.md`. The bass is the papers' square wave, not a file.

## 6. Measured (`scripts/measure-roll.mjs`, 30 Aug, headless Chromium at 48 kHz)

An analyser in front of the destination, every buffer source's start and buffer length recorded, every oscillator's start and frequency recorded, the bench's own loop origin exposed as `window.__benchLoopStart`.

- **Level.** Drums on the acoustic kit −16.6 dB mean over a pass, the electronic kit −17.9, the bass −15.8 (it sat at −13.5 before the input trim came down from 0.55 to 0.42). Every part within 2.1 dB.
- **Velocity.** The ghost hat in bar 2 (velocity 62) peaks at −20.9 dB, the accent hat (88) at −13.9: 7.0 dB apart. The kick (112) −17.2.
- **Bend.** The long A before the bend 110.0 Hz at either range. At range 2 the bottom of the bend is 98.5 Hz (a tone down is 98). At range 12, 80.0 Hz halfway (a tritone is 78) and 57.0 at the bottom (an octave is 55; the 93 ms frame spans the sweep). At range 24, 29.0 (two octaves is 27.5, at the detector's floor). The first run read every pitch 8% low because the harness assumed 44.1 kHz; the context runs at 48.
- **Quantise.** The roll as written: 85 hits, 56 distinct onsets in a pass. At 1/16 and 100%: 85 hits, 40 distinct onsets, 16 lost. At 1/32: 56, none lost.
- **Hold.** Under the wrong map, bar 2 beat 4.5 (the kick's pickup and the low tom, nothing else) books the crash (2.56 s) and the open hat (1.36 s); with the example held it books the kick (0.408 s) and the low tom (0.434 s). Same at bar 4.
- **Restart.** 57 hits in a pass before a stop and after a start.

## 8. Held, and what is not built

- **Unwalked by Mike.** As Valve, Plate, the Edit bench, the Balance Desk and the Lane.
- **One part at a time.** The paper bounces the drums and the bass separately, so the bench plays one. Both together would need a balance and would hide what a velocity does.
- **The bend lane is read-only.** Its points are the file's; the range is the control. Drawing a bend is the Automation Lane's job and the Connections tab says so.
- **Swing** is named in the Reference and not built; strength is the percentage quantise the 2025 scheme names.
- **The Learn chapters** are untouched; the Learn lesson's outro pointer moved from the retired page's id to `piano-roll` so it resolves.
