## 1. What the topic is, and what the exam does with it

Reverb is a space's answer to a sound: the dry event, a gap, the first reflections, then a tail that falls away. The written paper asks a candidate to describe that tail in the scheme's four words and two numbers. 2019 Q3(a) drew three dials and asked for the settings that would recreate the reverb on a vocal: "Type: Hall. Pre-delay: accept any value between 200ms-400ms. Reverb time: accept any value between 2.5s-4s." 2021 Q1(d) tabled it: "Reverb type: hall/plate/cathedral/church. Reverb time: within 1.5 to 3 secs. Wet level %: accept any value between 10-30." 2024 Q1(c)(i) credited "long reverb time (1.5–4 secs)/large size; medium/long pre-delay; ... medium/high wet send; ... stereo". Across nine years the reverb-time evidence clusters between 1.5 s and 4 s; pre-delay is given a number once (200 to 400 ms, for a hall); wet level once (10 to 30 per cent). There is no mark-scheme number for RT60 as a room measurement, for diffusion, damping or early-reflection level, so the bench shows none as a target.

The practical paper asks the candidate to set it. "Apply reverb to the vocals. Use a 2 second reverb. The reverb must be clearly audible but not swamp the vocals" (2019 AS 5(a); the scheme: "2 second reverb used on entire vocal (1). Suitable send level ... (1). Bass and keyboard parts not affected (1)"); "a 3 second reverb" that "should blend the vocal with the electric guitar" (2019 A 5(a)); "a 1.5 second reverb" (2024 AS 5(a)); the gated rhythm at "approximately 5 seconds" (2020 A 5(e)); "Stereo reverb is present throughout ... [award 0 if reverb is mono]" and "Dry level decreases to -∞ through 22-25, leaving the wet level constant" (2025 A 5(d)).

The reports say where the marks are lost. 2018: "The amount was the common problem, vocals being too wet or even completely swamped." 2023 AS: "most because they used reverb on a channel insert so it panned with vocals. It's surprising that after many years of highlighting this as bad practice it still happens." 2019 A: "Sometimes the reverb was gated as well as the vocal, proving that the candidate hadn't used an aux for the reverb". 2023 A: "Most either applied a static reverb or simply automated the wet/dry balance. But those with more advanced analytical skills noticed that it was the reverb time that was increasing rather than the wet amount." 2023 C3 (Funkytown): "Many recognised the dry signal disappeared, leaving only the wet; a significant number incorrectly stated the wet signal had got louder or the reverb was longer." 2024 C3: "Some candidates were too vague with responses such as 'the reverb was a bit shorter'." 2022 A: "many examples where candidates had incorrectly used reverb to create more sustain". 2020 A Q6: "Many students confused gating with compression"; "Very few candidates commented on the routing, e.g. insert order, reverb as inserts." 2023 C3 Q2(a)(ii): "Very few were aware of pre-fade sends."

## 2. The judgement: the tail is the picture, the dials are the paper's

The console is the paper's own answer sheet. 2019 drew Type, Pre-delay (ms) and Reverb time (s) as dials; 2021 added Wet level %. So the console is Play · Source · Type · Pre-delay · Reverb time · Wet · Hear, every dial a real rotary with the exam's word, its unit and its value in the mono face, and the More row adds what the practical paper marks: Damping (Bright to Dark), Stereo / Mono, Routing (Send / Insert), Pan, Dry.

- **Sources** (chips): Vocal, Guitar, Snare. The vocal is the paper's subject in nine of the eleven practical tasks; the guitar is the spring's; the snare is the plate's and the gate's. The snare is a one-shot fired on beats 2 and 4 at 100 bpm (every 1.2 s); the vocal and the guitar are phrases followed by silence, so the tail is heard on its own.
- **Type** (chips, the spec's six in the spec's order): Room · Hall · Plate · Spring · Gated · Reversed. A type is the shape of the answer: where its first reflections fall, how dense it starts, how it ends.
- **Pre-delay**: 0 to 400 ms, linear, so the 2019 scheme's 200 to 400 ms is on the dial and 5 ms is a real setting. **Reverb time**: 0.2 to 8 s on a log dial (three significant figures, law 25), so 1.5, 2, 3 and 5 s land exactly. **Wet**: 0 to 100 per cent. **Dry** (More): 0 to 100 per cent, default 100, the channel fader, so 2025's "dry level decreases to −∞, leaving the wet level constant" is a move the student makes and hears. **Damping** (More): 0 to 100 per cent, Bright to Dark. **Stereo / Mono**, **Send / Insert**, **Pan** (L to R): the three routing facts the reports name.
- **The graph** (`lib/bench/reverb-model.js` makes every number, `ReverbBench.jsx` plays it): source → dry gain → dry panner → master; source → wet gain → pre-delay (a DelayNode, 0 to 0.4 s) → convolver → wet panner → master; master → a ceiling compressor → destination. Two convolvers alternate: a change of type, time, damping or stereo builds a new impulse response into the idle one and crossfades 60 ms, so nothing clicks. Pre-delay, wet, dry and pan write through `setTargetAtTime`. Routing: on Send, the wet panner stays centred and the reverb stays stereo whatever the Pan does (the return is its own channel); on Insert, the wet follows the Pan and Stereo/Mono, because the reverb sits in the vocal's channel and goes where the vocal goes. That is the 2023 AS fault, heard.
- **The impulse response IS the model.** `impulse({ type, time, damping, stereo }, sampleRate)` returns the two channels the convolver holds and the envelope the stage draws, so law 6 holds by construction. Length is the time to −72 dB (1.2 × reverb time), capped at 8 s. The tail is decorrelated noise per channel (the same channel twice for Mono) under `exp(−6.91 t / time)`, which is −60 dB at the reverb time by definition of RT60; Damping splits the noise at 2 kHz and lets the high band decay faster, `time × (1 − 0.75 × damping)`. Early reflections by type: Room eight taps within 3 to 35 ms, dense and loud; Hall six taps spread 15 to 80 ms, quieter, the tail arriving after; Plate none, dense from the first sample and bright; Spring a pulse train every 55 ms, each pulse a short downward chirp (the coil's dispersion), decaying at the reverb time; Gated the room's start with the tail cut at a 120 ms hold and a 10 ms close; Reversed the hall's answer played backwards, a swell that stops at the reverb time. Every response is normalised to unit energy, then trimmed per type by the harness so Wet at 100 sits with Dry at 100 within 3 dB.
- **The stage's handle is the dial** (law 26): the tail's end where it meets the −60 dB floor is a drag handle; drag it right and Reverb time lengthens, the canvas reports `data-rt60` equal to the console's readout. The pre-delay gap is a second handle, `data-predelay`.

Console arithmetic at 1280: Play 104 + Source 130 + Type 200 + Pre-delay 88 + Reverb time 88 + Wet 88 + Hear 290 = 988, so the Hear column has room and no `.meaning` line wraps.

## 3. Three jobs, three pictures

- **Core, `data-stage=tail`: the tail, described.** A dB-against-time plot the way the spec asks it to be described: the dry event as a filled mark at zero, the pre-delay gap bracketed in ms, the early reflections as spikes, the tail as the answer's envelope in the accent falling to a −60 dB floor line named as such, the reverb time bracketed in seconds at the crossing. The wet dial is the tail's height against the dry mark. While the bench plays, the source's own envelope runs across the plot dotted, so a phrase and its tail are seen to overlap or not. The line names what you hear in the scheme's words (the source, the type, the time, the pre-delay, the wet, and the word the schemes use: dry, short, long, wet, swamped, gated) and says what to try.
- **A-level, `data-stage=path`: the paper's signal path, judged.** The 2020 Q6 figure's idiom and the 2023 task's: the source's channel drawn as CHANNEL → INSERT (the reverb, or empty) → PAN → FADER (Dry) → MIX, with SEND (Wet) → REVERB RETURN → MIX beneath, the return unit carrying Type, Pre-delay, Time and Damping in the paper's words, the return's width marked Stereo or Mono. Each box carries a verdict for the job (suits, partly, does not suit). Touch a control and the line judges that section: AO3 the setting and its effect, AO4 the verdict, the better setting, and the report that says so. With a paper's task set, the line is the scheme's own points with its year, "as directed" or "not yet: <the point missed>".
- **Extension, `data-stage=machine`: the machine, opened.** Three lanes in time: DRY (the source's samples), THE ANSWER (the impulse response as its real samples: the gap, the first reflections labelled with their milliseconds, the noise tail, its high band drawn as a second thin line fading first under damping), WET (the dry convolved with the answer, what is heard). The line opens the machine: a reverb is a space's answer to one clap; every sample of the dry sound stamps a copy of the answer and the wet signal is all the copies added, which is what a convolution reverb does with a recorded answer and what this bench does with a made one; then the type's mechanism in a sentence (a plate has no first reflections because a sheet has no walls; a spring sends the answer back as a train of pulses; a gate closes the tail at its hold, and the 2020 report's "many students confused gating with compression" is the line's own warning; reversed is the answer played backwards).

## 4. Presets

Core shows sounds a student can hear the difference between; A-level and Extension show the papers' tasks and the Judge patches; state carries across levels (the Synth bench's rule of 2 Sep).

Core: **Small room** (Vocal · Room · 0.5 s · 5 ms · 20 %), **Hall** (Vocal · Hall · 2.6 s · 40 ms · 25 %, the bench opens here), **Plate** (Snare · Plate · 1.6 s · 0 ms · 35 %), **Spring** (Guitar · Spring · 1.8 s · 0 ms · 30 %), **Gated** (Snare · Gated · 4 s · 0 ms · 45 %), **Reverse** (Vocal · Reversed · 2 s · 0 ms · 40 %).

A-level and Extension: **2019 AS paper** (Vocal · Hall · 2.0 s · 20 ms · 25 % · Send · Stereo; the scheme's "2 second reverb ... Suitable send level"), **2019 paper** (Vocal · Hall · 3.0 s · 30 ms · 30 %; "a 3 second reverb"), **2020 paper** (Snare · Gated · 5.0 s · 0 ms · 50 %; "very long at approximately 5 seconds" and gated), **2019 dials** (Vocal · Hall · 3.2 s · 300 ms · 25 %; the C3 Q3(a) answer inside "200ms-400ms" and "2.5s-4s"), **Judge: swamped** (Vocal · Hall · 4.0 s · 40 ms · 70 %; the 2018 report), **Judge: an insert** (Vocal · Room · 1.0 s · 10 ms · 30 % · Insert · Pan hard left; the 2023 AS report), **Judge: mono** (Vocal · Hall · 2.0 s · 20 ms · 25 % · Mono; 2025's "award 0 if reverb is mono").

The judge's bands, said as the bench's own where no scheme gives one: time within 15 per cent of a directed figure; "clearly audible but not swamped" is Wet between 12 and 40 per cent, with 2021's "10-30" quoted for a verse vocal; below 8 per cent "too dry" (2018: "Short reverbs occurred but only occasionally. No reverb at all was rare"); above 50 per cent "swamped"; Stereo required on any vocal task; Send required where a gate or a pan is in play; pre-delay judged only on the 2019 dials (200 to 400 ms). Each task preset lands "as directed" and each point breaks the way the reports describe (mono, an insert that pans, too wet, a 1 s reverb where 2 s was asked). Pinned by tests in `tests/bench-reverb-model.test.mjs` and the three levels' copy by `tests/bench-reverb-depth.test.mjs` (a `noDash` assertion on every line).

**What cost a round.** The judge's first cut faulted a 0.5 s room on a vocal, a 5 ms pre-delay and a gated snare at 45 per cent wet, none of which any scheme or report faults, so the Core presets failed their own test until the bands were taken back to the evidence. Truncating the impulse response was not a gate: a snare that rings keeps answering, so the return got its own gate, opened when the answer arrives and shut 130 ms later. The listening harness's RT60 fit needs one hit alone and fires four; that is recorded, not hidden.

## 8. Held, and what is not built

The side-chained rhythmic gate (2020 A 5(e)) is not modelled: the bench's gate closes on a fixed hold, and the side-chain is the Dynamics bench's subject. The reverb-time automation of 2023 A 5(f) is a candidate target for the Automation Lane, not a bench control. The answer is synthesised, and the Reference tab says so beside the convolution sentence; a recorded space is a later swap of one function. The history the 2022 essay asks for (natural, chamber, spring, plate, digital, convolution) stays with Inside the Room's hardware bench and the Learn chapters; the bench names the mechanism of each type it plays and no more.

## 9. The critique pass (2 Sep 2026, evening, two fresh readers on the local bench)

A student-walk reader and a fact-checker, both read-only, before the bench was called ready. What they found and what was done:

- **The paper presets narrated settings the patch did not have.** A met scheme point was worded as a miss ("on an insert ... in mono" on a stereo send), in the sentence a student is told to copy. Every point now has a met and an unmet wording, pinned by a test.
- **A section's verdict was painted on every box in it.** A centred pan and a full fader read "does not suit" under a mono return or a swamped send. Each box is now judged for what it holds (`boxGrade`), and the return box's touch judges whichever of type, time and pre-delay is at fault.
- **Nine attribution drifts and a mid-sentence quote.** "The 2018 report" became "the 2018 AS report" and so on for every year that holds more than one paper; the 2019 A quote runs to its clause; the 2022 scheme's "Gated reverb" is credited to the scheme, not the paper; the time band is the 2024 scheme's, not "every scheme's"; the 2025 mono zero is one mark, not the question; the Funkytown report is quoted the right way round.
- **The bench's own numbers are named as its own.** The gate's hold, the spring's pulse, the reflections' times and the 2 kHz split are said in the Reference tab to be numbers chosen to be heard, not figures a scheme gives; the send-level band is called the bench's own where the judge uses it.
- **Reversed and gated copy.** A reversed answer no longer reports first reflections at 2,520 ms or a gap "first"; a gated answer's Hear stat is the gate's shut time, and its Extension line no longer claims a 60 dB fall.
- **The first 120 ms, magnified.** At the plot's scale a 40 ms gap was fifteen pixels and six reflections six hairlines, so Core carries an inset of the first 120 ms with the gap and the spikes readable; the Core legend moved to the top, off the tail's end; the dry envelope is fainter; the axis reads "500 ms" not "500"; the verdict under the INSERT box no longer prints through "SEND AND RETURN" when the More row shortens the stage.
- **Held for a second pass (recorded, not hidden):** the lines run longer than law 11's sentence length in Teacher mode (the Synth bench's do too, and Mike walked those); Extension has no presets of its own; there is no side-by-side comparison of two types on one source; the A-level picture has no tail in it; "hold: dry" is the bypass and is now said to be, but not renamed; pass and fail are carried by the tail colour and gold as well as by the word.
