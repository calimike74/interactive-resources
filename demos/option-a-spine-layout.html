Effects Processing

Equalisation (EQ)

How EQ shapes frequency balance — from broad tonal control to surgical precision.

7 sections
01

What EQ Solves

Every sound contains a mix of frequencies. Some are too loud, others too quiet. EQ lets you boost or cut specific frequency ranges to shape the tonal balance of a signal — fixing problems or enhancing character.

02

Graphic EQ

A graphic EQ splits the frequency spectrum into fixed bands — typically 10 or 31. Each band has a slider that boosts or cuts at that specific frequency. The slider positions give you a visual "graph" of your EQ curve. Quick to use, but you can only adjust the frequencies it gives you.

03

Frequency Bands & Spacing

Bands are spaced at octave intervals (10-band) or third-octave intervals (31-band). An octave means doubling the frequency — so bands go 125Hz, 250Hz, 500Hz, 1kHz, 2kHz, 4kHz. More bands means finer control, but the frequencies are still fixed.

Octave Bands animation
04

Parametric EQ

A parametric EQ gives you full control over each band: choose any frequency, set the gain (boost/cut), and adjust the Q (bandwidth). Typically 4–7 bands, each fully sweepable. More surgical than graphic EQ — you target exactly the frequency you need.

4

05

Q Factor (Bandwidth)

Q controls how wide or narrow the EQ band is. Low Q = wide and gentle, affecting many frequencies around the target. High Q = narrow and precise, targeting a specific frequency. This is the key parameter that graphic EQ lacks.

06

Signal Routing

Graphic EQ uses parallel routing — all bands process the signal simultaneously. Parametric EQ uses series routing — the signal passes through each band in sequence. Series routing means each band adds cumulative phase shift, which is why linear-phase EQs exist for mastering.

6
Signal Routing animation
07

When to Use Each

Graphic EQ excels at quick, broad tonal adjustments — live sound, DJ monitoring, room correction. Parametric EQ is the tool for precise, surgical work — removing resonances, shaping individual tracks in a mix, mastering. Most DAWs default to parametric because of its flexibility.
